Flight Time Truth

Can we trust the actual_elapsed_time column in US flight data? This report rebuilds each flight's duration from departure and arrival times and checks it against the reported value.

Data: US DOT Bureau of Transportation Statistics, Reporting Carrier On-Time Performance · Jan 1 – Mar 31, 2026 · 1,671,142 flights · 13 airlines · 346 airports

Answer: yes, the column is reliable. After correcting for time zones and overnight flights, the recalculated duration matches the reported one for 97.1% of completed flights. Every large gap had a technical cause, not a wrong value.

Cancelled and diverted flights have no departure or arrival time, so they can never match. Looking only at completed flights, the match rate is 97.1%.

Method

  1. Raw subtraction

    arr_time - dep_time fails because the times are HHMM numbers (1300 - 1255 = 45, but the real gap is 5 minutes) and are in local time.

  2. Proper data types

    MAKE_TIME(hhmm / 100, hhmm % 100, 0) turns clock values into TIME; MAKE_INTERVAL(mins => …) turns minutes into INTERVAL.

  3. One time zone

    Joined the airports table twice (origin and destination) and converted both times to UTC: local time − tz.

  4. Overnight flights

    233,665 flights (14%) land after midnight UTC, so their duration came out negative. Adding 24 hours fixed them.

  5. Explain what is left

    Checked the remaining mismatches: most of them are cancelled or diverted flights, which have no times at all.

Key takeaways

  • The reported flight duration can be trusted. Every large gap had a clear technical cause, not a data error.
  • Local times need care. Comparing times from different airports without a time zone gave wrong results for more than a third of all flights.
  • Data types matter. Treating HHMM clock values as plain numbers matched fewer than 2% of flights.
  • A low match rate is not proof of bad data. Every step of the check removed a technical cause, not a data error.

Limitations

  • The tz column holds one fixed UTC offset per airport, which does not change during the year.
  • About 2.8% of flights still do not match; this group was not analyzed further.
  • The data covers only one quarter (Q1 2026) of domestic US flights.
